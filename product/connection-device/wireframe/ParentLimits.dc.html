Parent
What you don’t see
None of it is a setting.
Her day. It is off unless she is holding it.
Anything word-for-word. No transcript exists.
Notes she recorded and kept to herself.
What she left off tonight’s list, or how often.
Her location, or who she is with.
Her mood. Nothing scores how she sounded.
A note she sends you is hers and reaches you whole. Everything else becomes tonight’s prompts on the device and is destroyed within two seconds — that audio never leaves it.
A screen with no controls on it. Competitors bury their limits in a privacy policy; stating them as product copy is the differentiation — and it is what a child gets shown when they ask what their parent can see.
